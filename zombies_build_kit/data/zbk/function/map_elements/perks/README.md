# Perks

Owns the reusable perk machines, Der Wunderfizz, purchase limits, acquisition order, player effects, and perk clearing. The nine implemented perks are Juggernog, Stamina Up, Speed Cola, Double Tap, Quick Revive, Mule Kick, Deadshot Daiquiri, Electric Cherry, and Widow's Wine.

## Lifecycle and state

`on_load` defines perk objectives and triggers. `initialize` clears runtime entities and player perk state, and `on_tick` and `on_tick_as_player` maintain machines, effects, and interactions. Each successful grant increments `perk_count` and the player's `perk_order`, then stores that acquisition position in the perk-specific score.

The ownership limit remains 4 across direct grants, cabinet purchases, and Wunderfizz claims. Duplicate grants leave the count and acquisition order unchanged. The [player actionbar](../../player/README.md#hud) reserves nine positions and reads acquisition scores from 1 through 9. Perks no longer occupy or clear hotbar slots.

## Responsibility folders

Each named perk folder owns placement, purchase, grant, sound, and sign updates. `wunderfizz/` owns random selection, claiming, presentation, and location movement. `management/` owns shared application, clearing, and bonus handling.

`deadshot/`, `widows_wine/`, and `electric_cherry/` own their egg detection, guarded ownership grants, and cabinet purchase entry points. Their spawn-egg authoring functions live under each perk's `build_kit/spawn_egg` path. Deadshot costs 1500 points with power on; downed players, duplicate ownership, insufficient points, and the four-perk cap block purchase. Electric Cherry costs 2000 points with the same purchase guards. Widow's Wine costs 4000 points with the same purchase guards. Player Tools uses `give_deadshot`, `give_cherry`, and `give_widows`; ownership is stored in `perk_deadshot`, `perk_cherry`, and `perk_widows`. These scores are cleared with the other perks on setup, reset, reload, and perk loss. Deadshot treats every non-explosive gun ray hit as a headshot, including shotgun pellets and piercing hits. It uses the existing weapon head multiplier, headshot kill tracking, and blood at the target head; body and leg splatter are suppressed for these hits. Explosive direct hits and splash keep their existing rules. Deadshot does not provide aiming assistance.

Electric Cherry emits one blue electric wave when a valid reload starts, before ammunition changes. Radius is 6 blocks multiplied by the fraction of magazine capacity spent, clamped to 0.25-6 blocks. Empty magazines, including one-round weapons, produce the maximum radius; repeated reloads do not accumulate radius. The three normal weapon slots use their own current and maximum ammo, including upgraded capacities. Shell-by-shell reloads emit once at the start. Full magazines, no reserve, an existing reload, and downed reloads produce no wave. Going down emits one 6-block wave before the downed team is assigned. Nearby living zombie piglins, including crawlers, reuse Dead Wire's nonlethal 3-second Slowness V and Glowing stun, with electric sparks. Element-immune, combat-ignored, turned, and decoy entities are excluded. Cherry-stunned zombies temporarily use a blue outline and emit two small blue dust particles every ten ticks. The blue team assignment is limited to unteamed zombies and released on stun expiry, cleared glow, death, or initialization. Custom map teams and external team changes retain their own colors and membership. Repeated Cherry hits refresh the timer without increasing particle density. Waves do no damage and award no points; repeated stuns refresh the existing effect rather than increasing strength.

Widow's Wine shares the player's hand-grenade ammunition, capped at four. Acquisition raises capacity to four without adding ammunition. A damaging zombie-piglin hit consumes one grenade and emits a 6-block web wave; empty inventories and downed players cannot trigger it. Thrown grenades consume the same pool, use the burgundy webbed grenade model, stick on contact, and detonate after a 40-tick fuse. Sticking a zombie freezes it immediately. Detonation retains ordinary grenade damage and kill credit, skips crawler conversion, and webs surviving eligible zombies. Flying grenades retain the shared flight limit.

Webs last 400 ticks (20 seconds): targets within 2 blocks freeze completely, those within 4 blocks lose 75% movement speed, and those out to 6 blocks lose 40%. Reapplication refreshes duration and preserves the stronger restraint. Element-immune, combat-ignored, turned, and decoy zombies are excluded. A temporary cobweb block display rides each affected zombie; no world blocks change. The web's own movement modifier and display are removed on expiry, death, turning, or initialization, preserving other movement modifiers.

Each round adds two grenades up to four; the existing round-2 grant is counted once. Max Ammo fills the same four-charge inventory. A webbed zombie's death has a 10% chance to spawn a red spider pickup with a blue particle halo, separate from ordinary powerup drop limits. It expires after 600 loaded game ticks (30 seconds at 20 TPS), and perk initialization removes loaded spider pickups on datapack reload and game reset. Each pickup also saves the current `perk_epoch`; unloaded pickups from an earlier generation are discarded before particles or collection when their chunk returns. Unstamped pickups from older versions are discarded too. It restores one grenade only to the credited killer, who must be within 1.5 blocks, own Widow's Wine, not be downed, and have room. Spider pickups rotate two degrees per tick like normal powerups, using fixed billboarding so the spin remains visible. The halo emits four blue dust particles every five ticks and stops when the pickup is collected or removed. Each pickup saves that player's ID at spawn; teammates cannot collect it, even when closer. Custom weapons supply their kill credit, and native melee uses the victim's attacker. Deaths without player credit produce no spider. A death can roll only once. Knife hits have a 20% chance to apply a full-strength web without consuming ammunition. While the knife is held, a separate threefold attack-damage modifier preserves the round-scaled knife, Bowie bonus, and independent damage buffs; the ordinary knife kills standard zombies in one hit through round 3. Switching items or losing the perk removes only this modifier. Sword Flay has no core implementation yet; independent damage multipliers are preserved for its integration.

Der Wunderfizz cycles nine entries (0-8), skips already owned perks, and supplies each perk's matching bottle and name. Deadshot is entry 6, Electric Cherry is 7, and Widow's Wine is 8. The existing 1500-point cost remains. Claiming rechecks the four-perk cap in case a direct grant filled the last slot during the roll.

Persistent placement markers are the source of truth. `initialize` resets perk state and rebuilds Wunderfizz UI from them. Public purchase and grant functions remain guarded by the owning perk state and the shared limit.

## Crouch bonus

Each model perk cabinet grants exactly 100 points to the first eligible player crouching within two blocks, once per game. The bonus is shared across players but independent for every placed cabinet, including multiple copies of the same perk. Wunderfizz is excluded. Players must be in Adventure mode and not downed; power, perk ownership, and Double Points do not change the bonus. If multiple players qualify on the same tick, the nearest receives it.

The claim lives in the persistent cabinet marker's `pm_bonus_epoch` score, compared with `#current perk_epoch`, so rebuilding its displays does not restore the bonus. Perk initialization advances the generation on game reset and reload, making bonuses available even for cabinets that were unloaded during reset. New cabinets start unclaimed. Claimed cabinets skip player proximity checks while their generation matches; claim tags remain presentation/debug state rather than the authority. Legacy bonus markers use the same generation check. Standalone legacy bonus markers remain supported; those within two blocks of a model cabinet are suppressed to prevent a duplicate reward.

## Model machines and collision

Spawn eggs place the ten cabinet models through `zbk:map_elements/perks/machines/placement/` using the matching base resource pack. The six perks retain their existing prices and purchase rules; Der Wunderfizz retains its random-perk cycle and location system. Deadshot Daiquiri, Widow's Wine, and Electric Cherry cabinets place, rebuild, collide, and delete like the others. Right-click a cabinet to buy, or use the Build Manager to open its delete dialog.

The Build Kit perk dialogs offer a spawn egg for every cabinet (`give_<perk>_egg` triggers, including `give_deadshot_egg`, `give_widows_wine_egg`, and `give_electric_cherry_egg`) and direct grants for the original six perks. Player Tools > Perks - Grant and Perk Items additionally offer Deadshot, Electric Cherry, and Widow's Wine, subject to the same four-perk cap.

Cabinet item displays use `view_range:0.35f`, matching the mystery box. This is a client entity-distance multiplier, not a distance in blocks. Placement and reconstruction apply it; reload or reset rebuilds loaded v2 cabinets with the reduced range, including displays temporarily hidden for diagnosis. Label ranges are independent.

New markers carry `pm_v2`, plus `perk_machine` and the perk-specific tag (`perk_juggernog`, `perk_stamina_up`, `perk_speed_cola`, `perk_double_tap`, `perk_quick_revive`, `perk_mule_kick`, `perk_deadshot`, `perk_widows_wine`, `perk_electric_cherry`), or `wunderfizz`. Their displays, interactions, and labels carry `pm_v2_runtime` and share the marker's `pm_v2_id` score. Bottle item IDs remain `zbk:perks/<perk>`; cabinet item IDs are `zbk:map_elements/perks/<perk>`.

Placement snaps to a block center and the nearest cardinal facing. Cabinet displays rotate 180 degrees from that saved facing so their fronts face the placing player; rebuilding also corrects existing v2 displays. It requires two clear blocks above the floor (air, or the invisible light blocks maps use for lighting; see the `zbk:perk_machine_clear` block tag) and reserves them as a two-block-high barrier column inside the cabinet, replacing any light block there. Initialization rebuilds owned entities and restores missing barriers without replacing other block types. Cabinet collision stays within the central block column. Reload removes previously owned side panes, preserving replacement blocks and pre-existing map panes. Delete removes owned entities and the central barriers.

Standard machine labels sit on the lower cabinet front, one block above the floor and 0.04 blocks in front of each cabinet surface (0.350 to 0.636 blocks forward from the marker), and stay aligned with the machine instead of turning toward the camera. Deadshot places its label 0.48 blocks above the floor and 0.64 blocks forward from the marker to clear the dispenser, with Deadshot, Daiquiri, and the price on separate lines below the cabinet sign. New machine interaction boxes are 1.25 blocks wide and 2.4 blocks tall, starting at the floor. Each client shows them only at close range (2.5 blocks at the default entity-distance setting), independently of other nearby players. Wunderfizz places its price, selected-perk name, and bottle at the same position beneath the nozzle: 1.25 blocks above the floor and 0.0875 blocks toward the back from the marker. The bottle keeps the cabinet facing throughout cycling and claiming instead of turning toward the camera. Wunderfizz retains its power, active-location, and purchase-cycle visibility rules. Reload rebuilds saved v2 labels at the new position.

Existing block machines are tagged `perk_machine_legacy` during initialization and remain usable. Deadshot Daiquiri, Widow's Wine, and Electric Cherry never had block versions, so they have no legacy templates or cleanup. They are not automatically converted to v2. Both generations retain Build Manager deletion: legacy cleanup checks the old machine's saved orientation, block types, and entity positions instead of placing an empty template over neighboring builds. New machine entities are excluded from legacy cleanup. Select a machine again if its dialog target has been removed or is more than six blocks away.

The older transitional `pm_marker` restoration remains separate from `pm_v2`; it cannot replace new machines with structures. Runtime model exports do not include Blockbench authoring projects.

To opt an existing block placement into conversion, tag its marker `pm_v2_upgrade` and initialize the perk module with that chunk loaded. Conversion uses its saved `playerYaw` and the bounded legacy cleanup, preserves its UUID, and removes the upgrade tag after success. Markers without a saved orientation are left pending. Untagged legacy machines remain unchanged.

## Validation

From the datapack repository, run `python tools/test_perk_machines.py --server-jar PATH --java PATH` with a Minecraft 26.2 server JAR and Java 25. The disposable local server checks placement, collision blocks, reset/reload, selected deletion, legacy cleanup, purchase routing, and purchase guards. Check model rendering, right-click targeting, and walking collision in a client with the matching resource pack before releasing a map.

## Authoring ownership

Feature-specific editor functions and Build Manager handlers live inside the owning gameplay feature's `build_kit/` folder. The shared Build Manager only owns tool input, pending selection, and routing; each feature preserves its own dialog context and cleanup order.
